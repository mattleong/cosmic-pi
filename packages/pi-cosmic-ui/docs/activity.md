# Activity

The shared Activity view behind `/activity`, `/subagents`, and `/tasks`, and the protocol producers use to publish into it. See the [README](../README.md) for an overview. [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md#activity) maps ownership and links the grouping, retention, and allocation implementations.

## Activity view

`/activity`, `/subagents`, and `/tasks` open the same full-screen, two-pane manager titled **Activity**, with **Attention → Subagents → Tasks** sections: what needs the user comes first, and Attention holds standalone questions still waiting on someone. Empty sections are omitted, and a settled standalone question leaves Attention because its answer lives in the transcript. Opening selects the first item that needs your input. Otherwise ordinary `/activity` preserves the session selection and zoom when reopened, and the latter commands select matching standalone work first, or reveal a matching nested item; they do not filter other sections away, and an item that needs your input inside their section still comes first. When Activity is unavailable, `/subagents` and `/tasks` show a notice instead.

Nested work appears beneath its explicit owner. Queued agents show as queued until they start, and work cancelled before it started shows its producer's reason beside its row.

The persistent view normally uses up to eight rows, growing for visible failures up to half the terminal height so the conversation and editor stay visible. Overflow notices keep attention counts visible; very narrow views combine attention into a total rather than imply none. Running rows show their summary after the name as live progress, such as a task's latest output line or `running bash · sleep 25`, dropping it before the title when space is short. Finished detail leaves the persistent view, but a failure that finished in the last ten minutes stays countable on one muted line pointing to `/activity` until its preview or details have been on screen in Activity; the line retires on the clock, without waiting for another change. A "Needs your input" heading appears only for waiting items the checklist cannot show itself. While an input dock such as a questionnaire is on screen, its own waiting rows are left to the dock; a hidden request that offers a `resume` action shows its producer's summary, such as `/ask-user to resume`, as its status. Section headings appear only in the full-screen manager, where they lead with what needs attention so a clipped heading keeps it.

Rows lead with their title, followed by a quiet suffix: the agent's profile and the model it runs on (`scout → openai-codex/gpt-6-luna:high ⚡`), or `task` for commands (questions need none, since their glyph and status say what they are). Under the manager's `Tasks` or `Subagents` heading the kind goes unsaid, since the heading names it. As an agent row narrows, its live activity gives way first, then the model's qualifiers, then the profile, then the title shortens to a floor; `provider/model`, the elapsed time, the tree branch, and the glyph stay. Below the floor the manager moves the identity to a second line under the title, and the list keeps the selected row whole when it scrolls. They also show elapsed time and an explicitly awaited marker. A row's state is its glyph: words appear only for what the glyph doesn't say, such as `needs your input`, `waiting for reply`, or a blocked reason, never `queued`, `paused`, or `failed`, so an ended row's bare time (`✗ flaky build  6s`) is its run time. Details lead with the same profile and model before the status. The title counts the items the tree presents, including those inside folded branches but not settled standalone questions. Human input ("needs your input", the same words in the title, section rows, and the banner), parent waits, blocked work, and failures are counted separately; the manager's banner names only waiting items the list isn't showing; paused work is counted with live work rather than as attention. The preview sits beside the list on wide screens and beneath it on narrower ones, including a phone-sized 57×46; only a short narrow screen shows one pane at a time, where Enter opens the details. Use shared arrow/Vim navigation and page keys: `h`/Left collapses or returns to the parent row; `l`/Right expands, then inspects a source. Enter inspects a source and folds or unfolds a section heading, which is never a pane to enter; its summary still previews beside or beneath the list. The detail pane previews the selected source at once, loading its details and keeping the preview current while the tree has focus; inspected details stay put for reading until refreshed or followed. Running work opens following its newest output; anything else, including queued work, a question, or a failure, opens at its top so the question or cause reads first. Entering or leaving the detail pane keeps the line at its top where it was. Detail leads with the status, then, for a parent, a rollup of its direct children (their states counted, then each child's glyph, title, and latest summary, the rest counted on an `… n more` line), then the provider's sections such as a failure's cause, a report, or output. A preview shows each section's short form, such as one line per tool call; inspecting shows its fuller form, such as each call's output. `?` expands the hint bar into a full help panel, `z` zooms, and `w` cycles only through human-input attention; while anything needs your input, `w Next input` is in the default bar. `/` filters the tree by title, profile, model, kind, and status words (`failed`, `running`, `queued`), keeping the owners of each match and only sections with one; while typing, letters go to the filter rather than actions, Enter keeps the filter, and Esc clears it. The list heading counts the matches, an applied filter's Esc clears it before the next Esc closes, and an empty result says so. Section headings show their summary and any section-wide actions.

Each action has exactly one key, which the detail pane lists as `[x] Stop` and the hint bar names the same way: `x` stop, `i` interrupt, `u` resume, `m` reply/message, `e` rename, and `c` clear. Actions without a letter are numbered `1`–`9`, with `a` paging through more. A section-wide action, such as `Clear all finished`, is offered on its section heading; its key still runs it from any row of that section, where it is named only in `?` help. The hint bar shows the primary exit key only; other configured cancel keys appear in `?` help. When the bar is wider than the border, the border keeps exit and help and the other hints wrap between pairs into rows above it. A confirmation appears in a band beneath the list and preview, which stay in view; Esc or `q` declines it. An action is retargeted to the current revision only when the row still offers an identical action (same id, label, confirmation, and handoff), so unrelated progress doesn't void the choice. `r` refreshes lazy details without moving the viewed log slice; `t` toggles the Technical section: the provider's identity and diagnostic fields plus source identity and revision metadata. `f` follows the selected source from either pane, refreshing on every change and keeping the newest output in view; it is on when running work is selected, scrolling up turns it off, and it is offered only for work that has started and not finished. Selection changes, follow-off, and close revoke pending delivery and cancel the host request. Esc returns to the list, then closes; `q` closes immediately.

## Producer protocol

Producers import `registerActivityProvider` and `ActivityItem` from `pi-cosmic-ui/activity`. Register once per producer session with `pi.events`, the exact `ctx.sessionManager.getSessionId()`, a stable provider ID, a synchronous `snapshot()` returning plain summaries, and `invoke(itemId, actionId, revision, signal)`. Call `publish()` after changes and `dispose()` during session teardown. `parent: { providerId, itemId }` is explicit ownership, never an inference from labels or start times. `registerRevisionedActivityProvider` wraps this for producers whose details and actions must be current; it republishes on the supplied change sources and returns the disposer. Its action callback receives `invocationCurrent()` as its fourth argument: recheck it after user input or another await before changing source state. For tests, `fakeActivityHost` from `pi-cosmic-ui/activity/testing` answers discovery and acknowledges registration.

Every item includes a stable `id`, `kind`, `title`, `status`, and `revision`. Statuses distinguish pending, running, paused, stopping, blocked, needs-input, done, failed, and cancelled. Needs-input rows wait on a decision and show as attention, not warnings; paused rows are held work; blocked rows and failures are problems. Every needs-input item must supply `inputTarget: "user" | "parent"`; other statuses cannot carry it. Blocked rows may supply `blockedReason: "parent-review" | "file-access-review" | "file-access" | "write-containment"`; other statuses cannot carry a blocked reason. Optional `startedAt`, `endedAt`, and `updatedAt` are finite epoch milliseconds for elapsed/update information. Agents may supply `profile`, `model` (`provider/model`, the model actually used), and `modelDetail` (what qualifies it when there is room, such as effort, fast mode, or a fallback); rows render them as `profile → model:modelDetail`. Optional `summary`, static `detail`, and `{ id, label, confirmation?, handoff?, scope? }` actions must contain display-safe information, not credentials or raw provider errors. Destructive actions must supply a `confirmation` prompt that describes their scope, including affected descendants. Set `handoff: false` on actions that open no UI, such as stop, so they run with the manager open. Set `scope: "section"` on an action that acts on every item of its section rather than its own item, such as clearing all finished tasks; Activity then offers it on the section heading, invoking it through the first item there that offers it. Snapshots are limited to 512 items per provider and 16 actions per item, and details to 16,384 characters; `ACTIVITY_LIMITS`, exported from `pi-cosmic-ui/activity`, lists every bound the protocol enforces. The registration helpers publish the first 512 items and the first 16 actions of each item, so producers that need a different selection should clip to these bounds themselves. Use optional `getDetail(itemId, revision, signal)` for logs or other lazy detail. It resolves to a plain string, shown verbatim, or an `ActivityDetail`: ordered `sections` of `{ title?, body, expandedBody?, format? }` that lead with what the user came for (a failure's cause, a report, recent output), and optional `technical` `{ label, value }` fields such as IDs, PIDs, cursors, and paths, which appear only behind the Technical toggle. Use `format: "markdown"` only for agent-authored prose; logs stay verbatim. Keep a preview scannable with `expandedBody`: `body` is the short form shown while previewing, such as one line per tool call, and `expandedBody` the fuller form shown once the item is inspected, such as each call's output. Both count toward the detail limit. Malformed sections or fields are dropped individually; anything else is rejected. It runs when a source is selected, previewed while it changes, refreshed, or followed, never on render, resize, or theme invalidation. Revisions must change whenever the item or its available actions change. Keep them per item, for example a hash of the item's published fields, so one item's progress never makes another item's action stale.

Nested items are ordinary items whose `parent` names their owner; attention rolls up to owners and sections. [ActivityService](../src/activity/service.ts) validates snapshot consistency, such as unique item and action IDs; invalid publication withdraws rows, starting counts, and acknowledgement together. [Service tests](../tests/activity-service.test.ts) cover rejection and recovery, while [group summaries](../src/activity/group-summary.ts) derive display state from accepted data.

`onAvailability(available)` and `isAvailable()` report acknowledged replacement ownership, not merely that Cosmic UI was discovered: keep the old panel until availability becomes true and restore it when availability becomes false. RPC and headless sessions never acknowledge the TUI replacement. An envelope that bypasses the helpers and exceeds these bounds withdraws acknowledgement like an invalid one, and the next valid publication can recover.
